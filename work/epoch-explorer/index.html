Unsolicited study · Epoch AI data · November 2026

Redrawing a dense scatter plot for a phone

Epoch’s Notable AI models chart fits on a phone screen. The years since 2010 hold about 90% of its dots and get about a quarter of its width. This is an unsolicited study: Epoch did not commission it. I rebuilt the core chart from their CC BY dataset to show how I would give those years room.

Data
Epoch AI, Notable AI Models (CC BY 4.0)
Rows used
543 of 1,092: every model with a training-compute estimate
Built with
D3 v7, matplotlib, this site’s tokens
Scope
1 chart, 3 trend cards, 1 static twin
Observed
7 and 8 November 2026

01 · What I looked at

On a phone, 90% of the dots share a quarter of the plot

On 7 November 2026 I spent an evening with Epoch’s AI Models explorer, Trends dashboard and Data hub at 1440 and 390 pixels wide. On 8 November I tested the chart again with Chrome DevTools Protocol device emulation at 390×844 (3× pixel ratio, iPhone user agent) and at 1440×900, and measured what it draws. The chart gets the hard things right: dense data, uncertainty on the page, provenance on the figure. These notes are about density and the pages around it.

  1. At 390px the whole chart fits, and the deep-learning era gets a quarter of it.

    The full 1950–2025 axis renders, every label is whole, and nothing scrolls sideways. The problem is density. The years from 2010 on get about 72 of the plot’s ~300 px, roughly the right quarter (2010–2026 alone is about a fifth), yet they hold about 90% of the dots: 564 of 630 drawn circles. The “4.2 x/year” trend label is drawn over about 51 of them.

    epoch.ai/data/ai-models · default view · 390×844 device emulation · 8 November 2026

  2. The squeeze comes from the axis.

    At 1440px the same era gets 202 of about 898 px, the same share as on the phone. The linear 1950–2026 axis sets that proportion at every width. Epoch already has a one-tap answer: on mobile the settings collapse into a “Customize graph” button, and a “Jump to: All time / Deep Learning Era / 2020+” row just below the chart zooms to the dense years. At 390px that row sits about 130 px under the chart, below the fold at first paint.

    epoch.ai/data/ai-models · 390×844 and 1440×900 · 8 November 2026

  3. Where the frontier is densest, labels land on dots.

    At 1440px all 17 model labels are whole. The “4.2 x/year” trend label covers 17 dots in the frontier cloud, the corner a reader looks at first.

    epoch.ai/data/ai-models · default view · 1440×900 · 8 November 2026

  4. Two counts for the same database.

    The AI Models page says “over 3700 models”; a tile on the Data hub says “3,200+ ML models tracked”. Both were probably true at different moments, which is the kind of drift a number rendered from the data prevents.

    epoch.ai/data/ai-models · epoch.ai/data · 7 November 2026

  5. The Trends date undersells its data.

    The page header reads “Updated Feb. 5, 2026” while several cards cite data through Q3 2026. A date on each card, or the newest card’s date in the header, would say how fresh it is.

    epoch.ai/trends · 7 November 2026

What I kept

The claim-first title, the 90% interval stated in words, the All time / Deep learning era / 2020+ presets, a distinct mark for speculative estimates, and provenance on the figure itself. The palette and typefaces come from my site’s own tokens.

02 · The rebuilt chart

Every width opens on the years since 2010

Same data, log scale and presets as Epoch’s default view. Below 768px the controls move into a bottom sheet, the plot turns portrait, ticks thin out, labels drop to the top six and a tap opens the tooltip.

The rules behind it

Each behavior is a rule, so it would carry to the next explorer.

Labels
Candidates are the largest model in their rolling six-month window. They are taken in turn from equal time slots so labels spread along the frontier, and each is placed greedily at the first free spot around its dot, or skipped. A leader line appears only when a label sits more than 8px from its dot.
Color
Domain uses eight fixed slots, Language first and Other last. Organization and Country show the eight largest across the whole dataset plus a gray Other, so changing the range never repaints a survivor.
Trend
An OLS fit of log₁₀ FLOP against publication year, fitted to the visible range from 2010 on, and separately before 2010 in the All time view. The 90% interval is the slope ± t·SE; the band is the 90% confidence band of the fitted line.
Phone
Portrait plot, ticks spaced by pixel distance, six labels, a 34px tap radius to the nearest dot, the tooltip above the finger, and controls in a sheet with 44px targets. Every width opens on the deep-learning era, where 481 of the 543 points are; All time is one tap away.
Access
Focus the plot and use ← → to step through models by date. Table lists every model in the current view and sorts by any column; Data downloads that view as CSV with the license line in its header.

03 · A decision

Open on 2010–2026, keep All time one tap away

The alternative I rejected: keeping Epoch’s default, the full 1950–2026 axis, and fitting the phone layout around it.

Why: with a linear 1950–2026 axis, the years since 2010 get about a quarter of the width at 390px and at 1440px alike (observations 1 and 2), and no layout change gives them more. In this study’s data, 481 of the 543 models with a compute estimate are from 2010 on, and the chart’s title makes its claim about that period. So every width opens on the deep-learning era. All time sits in the same control, and when it is on, the chart fits the years before and after 2010 separately.

The cost: the 62 models from before 2010 are off-screen at first paint, and a reader who wants the long history has to ask for it.

04 · The stat-card grammar

One dataset, three framings

Epoch’s best interaction is the unit switch on its trend cards, which shows growth as an increase per year, a doubling time, or orders of magnitude (OOM) per year. Here it drives three cards computed live from the same JSON as the chart: the number, the sentence and the interval change together.

Show growth as

Before deep learning · 1950–2009

↑1.5× per year

From 1950 to 2009, training compute of notable models grew 1.5× per year.

90% CI 1.4× to 1.6× · n = 62 · OLS on log₁₀ FLOP

Deep learning era · 2010–2026

↑4.3× per year

Since 2010, training compute of notable models has grown 4.3× per year.

90% CI 4.0× to 4.7× · n = 481 · OLS on log₁₀ FLOP

Recent · 2020–2026

↑5.2× per year

Since 2020, training compute of notable models has grown 5.2× per year.

90% CI 4.3× to 6.3× · n = 330 · OLS on log₁₀ FLOP

The 2020+ interval is more than twice as wide as the 2010+ one (0.16 against 0.07 orders of magnitude a year): fewer years, more scatter. The interval on the card carries that caveat by itself.

The copy template

Each card is one sentence built from four parts: a lead that names the period, a subject, a verb phrase owned by the unit, and the number. Written down as a template, a researcher can add a card without a designer and every card says the same thing the same way.

The three units, from one fitted slope b (log₁₀ FLOP per year) and its 90% interval [lo, hi].
UnitNumber shownSentence90% interval
Increase/year10^b, one decimal, with ×{Lead}, {subject} has grown {n}× per year.10^lo× to 10^hi×
Doubling time12·log₁₀2 / b months, one decimal under 10{Lead}, {subject} has doubled every {n} months.Bounds swap: 12·log₁₀2 / hi to 12·log₁₀2 / lo
OOM/yearb, two decimals{Lead}, {subject} has grown by {n} orders of magnitude a year.lo to hi
  1. The lead names the period: “Since 2010”, “From 1950 to 2009”.
  2. Tense follows the period: “has grown” while it runs to the latest data, “grew” once it is closed.
  3. An arrow marks a rate of increase. A doubling time gets none, because a smaller number means faster growth.
  4. The interval travels with the unit, and its bounds swap for doubling time.
  5. b is the least-squares slope of log₁₀ FLOP on publication year; the 90% interval is b ± t(0.95, n−2)·SE(b).

05 · Same data, in Python

The same encoding, rendered with matplotlib

Researchers often need a static figure for a paper, a post or a slide. tools/epoch_chart.py reads the same JSON and this site’s tokens.css, so the PNG has the same colors, fit and label rule as the live chart and cannot drift from it.

The redrawn chart at the top of this page is its output, rendered at 2400 × 1440 (2x) with Geist and Geist Mono, once from the light token block and once from the dark one. The fit matches the live chart: 4.30× per year, 90% CI 3.97× to 4.65×, n = 481.

The excerpt below is the label rule. Each label tries eight spots around its dot and takes the first one that is free and covers at most one other dot. When none qualifies, it moves further out on a leader line; when that fails too, the label is skipped.

    # model labels: same candidate rule as the live chart; greedy, skip on collision
    to_px = ax.transData.transform
    offsets = [(6, -3.5, "left"), (-6, -3.5, "right"), (4, 5, "left"), (-4, 5, "right"), (4, -12, "left"),
               (-4, -12, "right"), (0, 9, "center"), (0, -17, "center")]
    leaders = [(dx, dy, ha) for d in (14, 22, 32) for dx, dy, ha in
               [(d, 0, "left"), (d * .7, d * .7, "left"), (0, d, "center"), (-d * .7, d * .7, "right"),
                (-d, 0, "right"), (-d * .7, -d * .7, "right"), (0, -d - 8, "center"), (d * .7, -d * .7, "left")]]
    count = 0
    for i in label_order(vis, args.labels, start, DATA_END):
        if count >= args.labels:
            break
        row = vis.loc[i]
        name = truncate(row.model)
        done = False
        for k, (dx, dy, ha) in enumerate(offsets + leaders):
            t = ax.annotate(name, (row.x, row.y), xytext=(dx, dy), textcoords="offset points", ha=ha,
                            fontsize=8.8, fontweight=500, color=T["ink-2"], zorder=6, path_effects=txt_halo)
            bb = t.get_window_extent(rend)
            px, py = to_px((row.x, row.y))
            covered = sum(1 for qx, qy in to_px(vis[["x", "y"]].to_numpy())
                          if bb.x0 - 4 < qx < bb.x1 + 4 and bb.y0 - 4 < qy < bb.y1 + 4 and (qx, qy) != (px, py))
            if free(bb) and (covered <= 1 or k >= len(offsets)):
                placed.append(bb)

06 · What I would do next

Five rules the next explorer could inherit

  1. A 390px chart contract. Every figure declares its phone behavior (aspect, tick spacing, label budget, where the controls go), and a 390px device-emulation screenshot runs before deploy. This study is one worked contract.
  2. The label rule as a design-system token. A label budget per width, the 8px leader-line threshold and greedy placement, set once in the chart library so collisions stop being fixed page by page.
  3. One card type for the Data hub. Seventeen datasets as one card (name, update date, rows, license, formats), faceted by Drivers, Progress and Impacts, with search. It replaces four tiles, thirteen rows and a download list that repeats the same names a third time.
  4. Numbers rendered from the data. “Over 3,700 models” comes from the dataset’s row count at build time, so the AI Models page and the Data hub cannot disagree.
  5. Freshness on every card. Each trend card carries the date of its newest data point, and the page header shows the newest of those.
